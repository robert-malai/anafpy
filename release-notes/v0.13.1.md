# anafpy 0.13.1 — the driver's card stops going out undated

The UIT card is the document a driver carries, and until now most of them left
without a validity date on them. Not by oversight: ANAF serves `data_exp_uit`
from the `info` endpoint alone, and scopes that endpoint to the transport
**organizer**. A company that files its own declarations but hires a carrier
therefore reads back nothing — *"Nu exista informatii pentru aceasta
solicitare"* — and anafpy, holding to the rule that ANAF owns the expiry clock,
printed the transport date and stopped there.

The half of that rule which was wrong is who is authoritative. The window is not
ANAF's discretion — OUG 41/2022 art. 11 fixes it at 5 calendar days from the
declared transport date, 15 for intra-community acquisitions and the lohn,
call-off stock and transit-to-storage legs — and ANAF applies it mechanically:
its own documented example pairs a 24 June transport with a 29 June expiry.
Nothing was being protected by staying silent, because the alternative to a
derived date was never "no date". It was the dispatcher working one out in their
head, or the driver not knowing.

So the card now always prints a validity, and **says where it came from**. When
you pass ANAF's `data_exp_uit`, nothing changes: grey `UIT VALABIL PÂNĂ LA`, red
`A EXPIRAT LA` and the red banner once it lapses. When you have none, the card
derives the statutory window and renders it so it cannot be mistaken for ANAF's
word — amber throughout, captioned `VALABIL (ESTIMAT) PÂNĂ LA`, with the rule
named in the footer so the date can be checked rather than trusted. A lapsed
estimate says `PROBABIL EXPIRAT`, in amber, because red is a verdict and only
ANAF's date is one. The A4 detail document opens the value with `ESTIMAT —` and
spells out the count, the article, and why ANAF's own date is absent.

Where the sources leave room for doubt, the **shorter** window is encoded. DIE
gets 5 days rather than 15: only DIN is named in the guide and in the ordinance
text vendored here, and the two errors are not symmetric — an estimate that runs
long puts a driver on the road with a lapsed UIT, which is a contravention,
while one that runs short makes a card announce itself early and prompts a
check.

## Behaviour change worth reading before you upgrade

Despite the patch version, one behaviour changed. **`UitCard.is_expired()` now
answers from the resolved window**, so a card whose statutory window has passed
returns `True` where it previously returned `False`. Silence from an endpoint
you are not entitled to query was never evidence that a UIT still lives, but if
you branch on `is_expired()`, that branch now fires in cases it did not before.
`uit_expiry` is untouched — it still holds ANAF's value alone, verbatim, and is
never computed.

The new `UitCard.validity(today)` is where the two sources meet and the only
place to read a resolved window from. It returns `source` (`"anaf"` or
`"statutory"`) alongside `last_valid_day`, `first_expired_day`, `days` and
`expired`; ANAF's date always wins when present. The rule itself lives in
`anafpy.etransport.validity` as three pure functions, for callers who want the
window without a card.

## The window where it is worth more than on the card

Two places got the same table, because by the time a card exists the transport
date is fixed and the window is just a fact to relay:

- **`etransport_prepare_*` returns `uit_window`** — `days`, `from_day`,
  `last_valid_day`, `first_expired_day` — in the preview the human approves. The
  transport date is still changeable at that point and immutable the moment the
  vehicle moves, so a route that cannot realistically finish inside the window is
  worth catching before the confirmation token is spent. The workflow skill puts
  it in the approval table.
- **`etransport_nomenclature`'s operation types carry `validity_days`**, so the
  model reads the 5-vs-15 split off data rather than off a playbook's prose —
  the copy that would silently drift if the ordinance is ever amended.

The card and detail-document tools return a `validity` object with its `source`,
and the skill now relays an estimate as an estimate, naming ANAF's disclosure
rule, instead of reporting that the expiry could not be read. `anafpy etransport
card` prints the same thing on the terminal when it falls back.

## Also in this release

Two rendering fragilities surfaced while laying the longer captions out, and are
fixed for everything, not just the new text: **cell captions on the card and the
label column on the A4 document now shrink to fit**. The longer label had been
overprinting its own value — a bug that was one long word away regardless.

`design/` now renders **every** state of the card and the detail document from
the shipped renderer: three plate shapes across four validity states, plus a
domestic TTN for the shorter window, long party names, a filing with nothing
optional supplied, and the four A4 variants. It previously carried its own copy
of the layout, dating from before the renderer existed, which meant the gallery
could show a card the library never draws.

## Not yet verified

No live filing has been rendered end to end, and **no real `data_exp_uit` has
ever been round-tripped through this code** — both were already true before this
release. The statutory window is exercised against constructed declarations.

**DIE's window has not been read against the ordinance's own text.** It is
encoded as 5 days on the reasoning above; confirming it either way is the way to
close the question, and DESIGN.md §13 records it as open.
