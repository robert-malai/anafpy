# anafpy 0.8.0 — the code the driver carries

## Feature

**A filed UIT now has a document to travel with.** An e-Transport filing yields a UIT, and the code then has to reach the driver — who must be able to present it at a roadside check — and usually the partner company too. ANAF issues nothing for that leg, so in practice the code travels as a bare string in a chat message, or as whatever a company's invoicing software improvises. The new `anafpy[cards]` extra renders two documents from a filing anafpy already holds:

- **The driver card** — one PDF page at a phone's own aspect ratio (90×195 mm, the 19.5:9 of an iPhone or a Samsung), so opened full-screen the phone *is* the document: the UIT set large, a QR code, and the plates and dates a check asks for, each in its own captioned cell. Send it over WhatsApp; the companion `summary_text` is the chat message to paste alongside it, with the bare code on its first line — the copy path that works even where a phone's PDF viewer won't select text.
- **The detail document** — the whole filing on A4 for the partner company or your own records: parties, route, transport documents by type, the goods table with Romanian number formatting, and an *Observații* section for your own notes (a weight the shipper gave as approximate; never boilerplate).

They are PDFs rather than images deliberately: a PNG is pixels, and the code's whole purpose is to be copied into someone else's system. Every value on both documents is real selectable text, and the QR is drawn as vector rectangles, crisp at any zoom and print. The QR encodes the raw 16-character UIT and nothing else — there is no official ANAF QR format; this follows the de-facto convention decoded from a card produced by Romanian invoicing software.

Two boundaries are held throughout. The documents are **informative** — generated locally, not issued by ANAF, and they say so on their face; what makes the transport legal is the declaration in the system, and only ANAF's own `data_exp_uit` is ever printed as the validity (no expiry supplied → the card shows the transport date alone rather than a computed window, and a lapsed expiry renders a red **EXPIRAT** banner). And fiscal codes print **verbatim** — a foreign VAT number already carries its country prefix; anafpy never adds or strips one.

The same two shapes on three surfaces: `UitCard` + `render_card`/`render_details` in `anafpy.etransport` (the renderer sits behind the extra; without it, a clear `AnafConfigError` names the pip command), the CLI `anafpy etransport card` / `details`, and two MCP tools — `etransport_uit_card` and `etransport_uit_details`, one per artifact, both writing the PDF to a path you name through the existing never-silently-overwrite guard. The binary never enters the model's context; only the card's `summary_text` comes back as text, because it *is* a message.

**The `etransport-declare` skill now starts from the law, not the form.** A new compiled reference, [e-Transport — Legal framework](https://anafpy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/anaf-reference/etransport/legal/) (also served as the MCP resource `anafref://etransport/legal`), is built from ANAF's official 2025 guide and the consolidated OUG 41/2022 — both vendored. The skill's step 0 uses it to answer the question that precedes any filing: whether this transport must be declared at all (the art. 16 obligation and the exemptions), and whether the CIF at hand is the party the law obliges to declare. After a successful filing it relays the obligations that attach to the UIT — hand the code to the driver before the transport starts, the GPS duty, immutability — renders the driver card by default with the expiry taken from a fresh lookup, and re-renders it after a correction or vehicle change, when the old card has gone stale. The sanctions regime it summarises applies in full since 1 January 2026, which is why guidance-first is the right default now.

Two supporting changes ride along. The Romanian display labels of the e-Transport nomenclatures moved into the library (`anafpy.etransport.labels`) so the renderer and the MCP `etransport_nomenclature` tool share one home — and counties gained labels in the process (the schema documents them only by number, and `BISTRITA_NASAUD` is not what a document should print). And the card's fonts are vendored, subset OFL Noto faces (356 kB for all three): fpdf2's built-in fonts are Latin-1 only, so `ș` and `ț` are unrepresentable in them, and a Romanian document can't be at the mercy of whatever faces a machine happens to have.

## Not yet verified

**No live filing has been rendered end to end.** The renderers are exercised against constructed declarations and the layout was settled against two real-world reference documents, but no card has yet been produced from a declaration actually filed through `upload_document` — and `uit_expiry` has never been round-tripped from a real `data_exp_uit`, only from values passed in by hand. The next real transport closes both.

**The legal reference is compiled, not counsel.** Its facts are sourced from ANAF's own guide and the ordinance text, with provenance in the frontmatter, but the skill's step-0 guidance is a summary for orientation — the reference states its sources precisely so they can be checked, and ANAF's current texts remain authoritative.
