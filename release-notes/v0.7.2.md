# anafpy 0.7.2 — the clock that decides is ANAF's

A message listing is the most ordinary thing this library does, and it had a way of failing that had nothing to do with your data: if your machine's clock disagreed with ANAF's by **one second**, you got nothing back at all.

## Fix

**A 60-day window no longer rides both of ANAF's limits with zero tolerance.** `listaMesajePaginatieFactura` validates the window against the *server's* clock — `endTime` may not be in the future, `startTime` may not predate its 60-day floor — and anafpy computed both ends from `time.time()` on the calling machine. At `days=60` that window touches both limits exactly, so any drift, in either direction, failed the whole listing. Seen in the field, off by a second:

```
endTime = 30-07-2026 15:15:28 nu poate in viitor fata de momentul requestului = 30-07-2026 15:15:27
```

This is not an exotic condition. A scheduled sync that pulls the full retention window rides both boundaries on every run, and a Windows box whose `w32time` isn't disciplined drifts into it on its own — no misuse required, and the failure looks like an ANAF outage rather than a clock problem.

Two layers now, and they answer different sizes of the same problem.

**The `days` window is built inside ANAF's limits rather than on them** — 5 minutes of margin at the start, 60 seconds at the end. The two ends are deliberately not symmetric: what the start margin gives up is 60 days old and about to leave ANAF's retention anyway, while the end margin *is* a freshness trade, so it stays the smallest value that covers a drifting clock. In practice it costs nothing you'd notice — ANAF indexes list entries asynchronously, seconds to ~15 minutes after processing, so the newest minute is not where fresh filings live.

An explicit `start`/`end` window is **not** touched by any of this. You named those instants; silently moving them would be anafpy inventing a window you didn't ask for.

**Beyond the margin, ANAF's own clock does the correcting.** The rejection above quotes `momentul requestului` — the server's time, in Romania wall time, which in summer is three hours off UTC. anafpy reads it, rebuilds the window against that clock, and re-asks the page **once** per walk. That handles drift of any size without a configuration knob, and it applies to an explicit window too: that isn't second-guessing the caller, it's ANAF stating the correction (and even then only the `end` ANAF called future is pulled back — `start` stays where you put it).

The recognizer is narrow, in the style of the two wording classifiers already in the transport layer. The quoted timestamp must actually be present — the swagger's own examples of these faults state the phrase without one — and a second fault is not skew: it surfaces as the `AnafResponseError` it always was, as does any fault with no clock to read. One correction, then the error stands.

Recorded in the [reference](https://anafpy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/anaf-reference/efactura/api/) §3b, where the observed fault text and its wall-time format now live.

## Not yet verified

**A clock running behind ANAF's by more than five minutes still fails.** That direction trips the `startTime` limit instead, and the start-side fault has never been seen quoting a moment — so there is nothing to correct from, and the 5-minute margin is the entire tolerance on that side. Widening it costs almost nothing and is the lever if this ever shows up in the field; guessing a correction out of a message that says only "too old" is not something this library will do.

**No live test can conjure a skewed clock.** Both layers are covered by the mocked suite — margins pinned at their exact limits in each direction, the correction parsed from a recorded fault body, the retry firing at most once, the Romania-timezone parse pinned in summer *and* winter — but the fault itself was observed once, in production, on 2026-07-30. If ANAF stops quoting `momentul requestului`, the correction quietly stops applying and the margins are what's left, which is the direction this fails in on purpose.
